# Drink at Ron — illustration creation guidelines

These rules govern every future card illustration. They supersede the rejected dwarf study and any earlier directions to imitate a particular game's characters. The approved card back remains the reference for craftsmanship and palette; illustrations need their own original visual identity.

## Visual identity

Rich, sculptural, painted fantasy-card art remains the direction: the tactile warmth and exaggerated expressiveness associated with Hearthstone, with original character designs and compositions. Add goofy dungeon-crawl and tavern-party humor where relevant, taking Heroes of Barcadia and Munchkin as tonal references for absurd situations, oversized props, and visual punchlines—not character or drawing templates. The mouse study went too far toward quiet woodland storybook art. Use bold silhouettes, expressive poses, broad painted forms, warm highlights, and controlled detail. Use walnut brown, parchment gold, bronze, and muted teal or moss as a family, with restrained card-specific accents.

Do not copy recognizable characters, creatures, costumes, props, silhouettes, compositions, or insignia from Hearthstone/Warcraft, Lord of the Rings, Dungeons & Dragons, or another franchise. Those references communicate a broad fantasy mood, not a design library. No dwarf archetypes in this collection. Exaggerated armor and heroic proportions are welcome when independently designed and readable; avoid crowded battle scenes. The card frame already provides the ornate detail; the illustration should provide clarity.

## Composition before detail

- One primary subject and one immediately understandable action. Use at most one supporting prop unless the card genuinely requires more.
- Center the subject's important features. Keep the face, complete head/ears/hat, hands, and key prop inside the central 60% of the source image whenever possible. Leave approximately 20% expendable space at the edges, especially above the head.
- Compose for a landscape 3:2 source. The current small arched illustration window is approximately 1.4:1 overall, with additional space covered by the painted border. The live window is the final authority; source-image approval alone is insufficient.
- Show the entire essential silhouette. Do not use close-up portrait crops, edge-touching heads, or props pointing out of frame. Peripheral feet, tails, and scenery may crop only if the action and character still read comfortably.
- Keep subject and background clearly separated in value. Use two or three main color groups, a quiet backdrop, and limited background shapes. Avoid shelves of tiny bottles, crowds, confetti, particle clouds, dense scenery, intricate clothing, and tiny visual jokes.
- Large shapes first, surface texture second. No important detail should depend on zooming in. Text, rules, pack logos, decorative borders, and UI symbols are never baked into the illustration.

## Prompt template

Original rich painted fantasy-party card illustration, landscape 3:2, no text or frame. [One subject] performing [one clear action] with [one essential prop]. Subject and prop form a compact group centered in the middle 60% of the canvas, with generous headroom and expendable margins on every side. Whole head and key prop visible. Quiet [simple backdrop], broad painted shapes, strong subject/background separation, [two or three main colors]. Goofy expressive personality where appropriate, sculptural forms, warm dimensional lighting and hand-painted surface, readable at 120 pixels. Use the card-back reference only for palette and material warmth. Independently designed character and costume; no copied franchise designs, dwarves, crowded scenes, excessive tiny ornament, tiny props, letters, logos, or UI ornament.

## Review before accepting an image

1. Inspect the source and then the actual arched frame at Small phone and iPad sizes. Check top corners, headroom, face, hands, and prop after cropping.
2. Reduce the illustrated window to roughly 120px wide. The subject and action should still read immediately. Inspect at arm's length; physical-distance readability is a human review, not an automated pass.
3. Check in grayscale or squint: the main subject must separate from the background without relying on tiny highlights.
4. Compare to neighboring cards: detail density, warmth, scale, and visual weight should be consistent. Reject art that forces one-off card geometry or image positioning to rescue its composition.
5. Generate a revised composition when framing fails. Do not shrink rules, stretch the image, or crop off essential features to make it fit.
6. Keep the source PNG and exact prompt; export an appropriately sized WebP/AVIF, normally 768px wide and below the 500 KiB runtime image budget. Include it in validation and offline caching when approved for production.

## Pack identity is separate

Core uses a small four-point bronze diamond. Every registered pack supplies a distinct `logo` artwork path. The same logo appears in its selector tile, the active-pack legend, and card footers. Shapes must remain distinguishable at 28px; color is supporting information, not the only distinction. Pack marks belong to the UI, not the illustration. Category names remain authoring/filter metadata and are not printed above the action text.

## Tone clarification — September 14

Keep the cohesive Hearthstone-inspired rendering and materials. Originality is about specific character identity, costume, silhouette, composition, and symbols; it does not require abandoning the fantasy-card aesthetic. Humor should be readable in the main subject or action: a grand armored adventurer with a tiny cup, an overconfident failed spell, or an unexpectedly pompous tavern guest. Avoid making every card cute, tranquil, or woodland-themed.
